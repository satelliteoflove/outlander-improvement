# What players and reviewers said was wrong with Outlander

Web research done on 2026-11-12 by a research agent, on the question: besides the slow driving
sections, what about *Outlander* (Mindscape; SNES 1993, Genesis 1992-93) frustrated players?
The report below is the agent's, lightly tidied. Nothing in it has been checked against the
game's code yet.

## How much there is

Very little has been written about this game: about ten substantive write-ups. Only two are
contemporary or near it, and both arrived second-hand (Nintendo Power via Hardcore Gaming 101;
Mega's 20% via Wikipedia). The rest are retro reviews, one detailed GameFAQs FAQ and a page on
The Cutting Room Floor. No Reddit threads, no speedrun.com page, no romhacking discussion, and
no transcribed Super Play, NMS, Total!, EGM or GamePro review of the SNES version were found.
Several key pages were blocked (listed at the end). Where only a search-engine snippet of a
page was seen, not the page itself, it is marked "(snippet)".

**No source that could be read uses the words "hit detection" about this game.** What sources
describe is near it: aiming that is hard, collisions that cost a lot, and melee that needs
repeated presses "until the hits connect" (complaint 4, and the single-source details). The
8 fps frame rate is also barely mentioned: snesaday says "noticeable slowdown here and there",
and HG101 (snippet) says the Genesis version "can get choppy".

## Complaints several independent sources agree on

### 1. The on-foot sections are the weak half (both versions)

- Nintendo Power, as reported by HG101 (snippet): "not particularly impressed... especially
  due to the tedious on foot sections, though they enjoyed the driving."
- Sega-16 (Genesis, 6/10): "This one aspect of the game is pretty horrid"; the play is
  "wooden"; animation is "bland and lifeless"; environments "repeat themselves quite often."
- snesaday (SNES): "beyond tedious"; "found myself dreading the side-scrolling part."
- JustGamesRetro (SNES): "clearly not as polished as the driving portions"; fights "alternate
  between stupidly easy and inexcusably difficult."
- GameFAQs, Xerebus 3/10 (snippet): "poorly constructed side scrolling stages" with repeated
  enemy sprites.
- GameFAQs FAQ: "a major disappointment for many people who liked the driving portion."
- A Sega-16 reader comment calls the gunfights "the worst T2-like gun fights I've ever seen."

### 2. Clumsy or stiff controls (SNES mostly)

- JustGamesRetro: "Control is one of this title's weaker points."
  - Y is accelerate and the reviewer still "instinctively waste[s] bullets".
  - Brake on X is "awkwardly mapped".
  - Reverse on A "doesn't work unless you've already come to a full stop with X", and A also
    fires the missile.
  - B has "no use at all."
- Nerd Bacon: "The controls in both the driving and the walking parts are pretty clunky."
- Xerebus (snippet): "What little challenge can be mustered is derived almost entirely from
  poor controls."
- kingofcrusher, GameFAQs (snippet): "the control is kind of stiff so [the towns are] pretty
  tough, especially later on."
- Counterpoints: Sega-16 praised the Genesis controls; HG101 (snippet) says the SNES port
  "improved controls"; the FAQ says SNES in-car shooting was "easier, and much more
  productive."

### 3. Harsh difficulty, and collisions that cost too much (SNES harsher)

- snesaday: "Your car doesn't take a lot of punishment"; "a ridiculous number of barriers
  blocking the road."
- JustGamesRetro: "hitting things costs a surprising amount of health."
- Nerd Bacon: one mistake can total the car and end the game.
- The FAQ: SNES "damage values while driving are exponentially higher than the Genesis
  version"; a non-breaking piece of debris at full speed takes half your health.
- HG101 (snippet): the SNES car "takes much more damage from collisions", and the SNES version
  has more roadblocks in place of the Genesis jeeps.
- Metagalaxia (Portuguese, 6.5/10): "a curva é dura" (the difficulty curve is hard).
- The FAQ also says ramming bikers is easier on SNES (HG101 snippet agrees). So contact damage
  looks asymmetric: obstacles hurt a lot, while bikers can be rammed.

### 4. Aiming and target readability while driving

The nearest thing to hit-detection complaints.

- snesaday: the forward machine gun is "really hard to aim because of the muddy graphics."
- JustGamesRetro: enemy projectiles "blend in too well with the asphalt"; the machine guns are
  "weak, hard to aim"; the missile is wasted if no helicopter is present.
- Sega-16: lining up shots against fast bikers is hard. Watching the enemies makes you hit
  rocks on the bends; watching the road leaves you open to attack.
- The FAQ: the in-car shotgun has a slow firing rate; you must match speed with a side attacker
  and steer towards him before it lands; the SAM "sometimes won't fire, just alternate the
  buttons quickly until it does."

### 5. Ammo and resource starvation (both versions)

- snesaday: "unending waves of enemies and constantly depleting resources means you're going
  to run out of ammo."
- Sega-16: running out of bullets sends you to game over "quite a bit."
- Lunatic Obscurity (Mega Drive): skip towns and you "will quickly run out of fuel!"; running
  out forces a road stage, "much harder than the town ones"; some bikers there can only be
  killed with the shotgun, which has limited ammo.
- The FAQ: road stops have no boxes or barrels, so you depend on enemy drops, and you "can't
  leave until someone drops a tank of gas."
- Against this, JustGamesRetro: "I never ran out of gas."

### 6. Poisoned food and water (both versions)

- Lunatic Obscurity calls "poisoned water that reduces your health" unfair.
- JustGamesRetro finds toxic food frustrating after a costly fight.
- The FAQ: the Crossbowman "very often drops food thats poisonous". Only the Geiger counter
  item tells you which food is bad.

### 7. Repetitive, with no variety in scenery or stops (both versions)

- Nerd Bacon: "there is no variety in them whatsoever."
- JustGamesRetro: "the driving is boring and repetitive"; pit stops "look and play almost
  exactly the same no matter where you pull over."
- Lunatic Obscurity: "the scenery during the driving sections never changes."
- The FAQ: "road stop levels are all the same."
- Metagalaxia: "repetição" (repetition).

### 8. No explanation or goal, and towns are hard to notice (SNES)

- Nerd Bacon: "no instructions, descriptions, or details of any kind."
- snesaday: "Is there a goal? Is there an ending? I don't know!"
- JustGamesRetro: "there is no visual representation of said town" on SNES. The Genesis
  version has a "Welcome to TOWN" sign; the SNES version only flashes the hazard lights (the
  FAQ confirms this).

## Single-source complaints and details

- **Saves and lives.** JustGamesRetro: "you only get a save password every five or six
  towns." Xerebus (snippet): one life, no continues. The FAQ: continues are earned by score
  (the first around 10,000 points, the threshold "seems to increase steadily", at most 3), and
  you score nothing unless you stop in towns. Lunatic Obscurity: you start with no extra lives.
- **Shared health.** JustGamesRetro: "your car health and body health are one in the same", so
  you can lose more on foot than you gain. The FAQ says the same for Genesis (oil equals
  health, and food raises oil).
- **Town combat** (JustGamesRetro): the punk enemy reloads "so fast that you can't get near
  him"; ducking is safe but "makes you immobile"; at a pit stop, "the first one you see will,
  unquestionably throw a molotov."
- **Melee reach** (snippet, from a GameFAQs review whose author could not be confirmed):
  "crouch and punch. Your reach is much better than theirs and they can't shoot down so you're
  practically invincible." The FAQ recommends the same thing for every on-foot enemy.
- **Chain bikers** (FAQ): "keep punching rapidly until the hits connect". This suggests melee
  hits on passing bikers register unreliably. It is the closest thing to a hit-detection
  remark found.
- **Crossbowman** (FAQ): "fires crossbow repeatedly, never stops... he'll just back away
  farther and keep shooting."
- **Land mines** on road stops (FAQ): "a pain to see... white circles on the street." The
  advice is to jump over them.
- **Graphics.** snesaday: "weird graphical distortion from all the sprite scaling"; the
  painted road lines "warp". JustGamesRetro: "Road graphics are muddy at times." Nerd Bacon:
  "on the low-end for a Super Nintendo game."
- **Sound.** JustGamesRetro: "mediocre." Nerd Bacon: music "just okay", effects "just as bad,
  if not worse." Against this, Sega-16: "rock-solid" (Genesis).
- **Running out of gas.** Nerd Bacon says the game switches to walking "when the gas runs out,
  with no warning", and that one shot can end a run. The FAQ describes a low-gas lamp and a
  fuel meter but lists them as Genesis only, so on SNES "no warning" may be true.
- **Mega** (UK, March 1993): 20%. Only the score is known, via Wikipedia, and it is probably
  the Mega Drive version.
- **Unverified, seen only in a search engine's summary:** a GameFAQs review saying roadside
  "hurdles... mysteriously drain your petrol when hit"; and someone calling the SNES version
  "much slower moving" than the Genesis one.

## Added from the GameFAQs reviews, read in full

Chris read the two GameFAQs reviews that were blocked for the agent and pasted them in
(2026-11-12).

**"Outlanderishly Poor", 3/10, SNES** (the review the agent knew as Xerebus's). Beyond what
the snippets gave:

- "Someone has taken it upon themselves to put little hurdles along the road that
  mysteriously drain your petrol when hit." This was listed above as unverified; the review
  does say it. Whether hitting a roadblock really costs fuel, and not only health, can be
  checked in the code.
- "there are just so many cacti along the side of the road that it isn't funny, and each of
  them provides as much danger as a common enemy"; "most rocks on the road are considered to
  be a 'viable opponent.'" Roadside scenery as a main source of damage.
- "Towns themselves are rather hard to find, given that they won't actually appear on the
  screen as you pass by them. You have to rely 100% on your map, or randomly pulling over."
  Pulling over anywhere else lands you in a non-town stage: "absolutely terrible, as they
  serve no real purpose but to annoy."
- "You are given one life, and no continues", and "the absences of any sort of password or
  save feature in the game itself". This contradicts JustGamesRetro, who reports a password
  every five or six towns: the reviewer may never have got that far.
- The car "handles poorly"; "What little challenge can be mustered is derived almost entirely
  from poor controls" (said of the on-foot stages).
- The driving is put at 80% of the game and called repetitive; "a landscape that never quite
  seems to change".
- Sound: one good piece of music that "repeats. Over and over"; the same few sound effects
  used for everything.
- No mention of hit detection, and none of the frame rate.

**"Fun, but flawed Mad Max-style action", Genesis.** This is the same text as the Sega-16
review quoted above ("wooden", "pretty horrid", "rock-solid" audio), so the two count as one
source, not two. It praises the Genesis driving for its speed ("the game never slows down to
accommodate more action on the screen"), which is the contrast with the SNES version. It says
the game was meant to be a Mad Max game until the licence fell through.

**The Cutting Room Floor.** Chris checked the archived page: it describes only the SMEGHEAD
name and nothing else. (Fetching the live site got his IP address banned: do not send
automated requests to tcrf.net.)

## Added from Hardcore Gaming 101, read in full

Chris Rasa's article of 22 October 2016, from the Wayback Machine's copy of 10 February 2026
(<https://web.archive.org/web/20260210151447/https://www.hardcoregaming101.net/outlander/>).
It replaces the snippets used above.

- **On foot** is what "bring[s] the game to screeching halt": "almost no enemy variety";
  shotgun ammo is scarce, "so the best strategy tends to be crouching and just punching
  enemies in the legs repeatedly until they go away"; the jump is "more of a short hop
  forward". "The side scrolling areas, unfortunately, are identical to their Genesis
  counterparts": "twenty-four versions of the same three or four areas."
- **Length and monotony.** "While the game is monotonous, and would take about three or four
  hours to complete", it gives "a password at every fourth town you stop at" and continues by
  score; twenty-four towns; "extremely challenging towards the end."
- **The ending.** The last boss, Duster, fights "like every other enemy in the game": "one of
  the most anti-climatic bosses of any game". A unique truck near the end "barely puts up a
  fight".
- **Frame rate**, of the game in general: "while it can get choppy". Nothing more specific.
- **Nintendo Power** "was not particularly impressed with the game, especially due to the
  tedious on foot sections, though they enjoyed the driving action."
- **SNES against Genesis.** Counted as improvements on SNES: more colourful graphics; "much
  higher quality music", four songs; the car seen from behind, which makes ramming bikers
  "much easier"; separate buttons for the side shotgun (the triggers) and the forward guns,
  where Genesis had one button and "would often cause you to waste ammunition"; and reverse,
  which Genesis lacks (there you could "return to your car only to be stuck in a rock").
  Counted against SNES: the jeeps of the later Genesis levels are gone, "replaced with many
  more road blocks for you to crash into", and "your vehicle takes much more damage from
  collisions compared to the Genesis game. So despite the control improvements it's much more
  difficult to drive aggressively and enjoy the game's strengths." "Many fans stick with the
  Genesis version for being more tightly balanced."
- The article names the forward guns' button as B; the JustGamesRetro review says B does
  nothing and the project's own notes have X firing. One of the two reviews is wrong.
- No mention of hit detection.

## Bugs and checkable details for someone reading the code

- **Broken cheat** (TCRF, via snippet; the page itself would not load). Entering SMEGHEAD as a
  high-score name shows "CHEATED" and sets a bit. The game then returns to the Mindscape logo,
  which clears memory, so the cheat never takes effect. TCRF's fix is PAR `7E07E901` at boot,
  which puts the flag at `$7E:07E9`. With the flag on, Start on pad 2 refills health in the
  on-foot areas. STARBUG1 reportedly shows the ending credits.
- **Auto-enter** (FAQ, SNES only): within "2-3 inches" of the car, "you lose control of your
  character and he automaticly enters the car", so you can leave a town by accident. Genesis
  makes you walk to the door.
- **Pull-over trap** (FAQ, Genesis): stopping close to roadside debris can leave the car
  "stuck in or behind the debris" when you return. The SNES version has reverse, but only from
  a full stop.
- **Missile** (FAQ): the SAM "sometimes won't fire, just alternate the buttons quickly until
  it does."
- **Helicopter** (FAQ): on SNES its bombs "doesn't seem to do anything!" A bug, or the
  author's misreading?
- **Empty boxes** (FAQ): boxes and barrels are empty more often on SNES.
- **Dead buttons** (FAQ): on foot, B, L and R do nothing.
- **Unclear items** (FAQ): Tires, and the Oil bottle on SNES, have no visible effect.
- **SNES PAR codes from the FAQ**, which point at ROM locations:

  | Address | Effect |
  | --- | --- |
  | `81B67C` | infinite missiles |
  | `81B846`, `81B84F`, `81B854` | starting fuel, car ammo, handgun ammo |
  | `90885F` | infinite handgun ammo |
  | `908A11/12` | gas can value |
  | `908A3B/3C` | car ammo value |
  | `908A82` | handgun ammo value |
  | `908AC0` | food value |
  | `908AE3/E4` | water value |
  | `90908D` | on-foot health |
  | `008E45` | car health |

  Pickup values are in bank `$90`, alongside the on-foot code.

## The agent's own inference (not reported by anyone)

- "Hit detection" as a known complaint may be community shorthand rather than something
  written down. The documented forms are: hard-to-aim forward fire against muddy, scaled
  sprites; very costly obstacle collisions on SNES; and melee hits on passing bikers that need
  repeated presses.
- At about 8 fps, steps are coarse. Projectiles and obstacles cover large distances between
  frames, which could explain both the hard aiming and the surprising collisions. No source
  makes this link.
