Outlander for the SNES, with an SA-1 improvement patch. The original game is on one side and the patched game on the other, both running in the Mesen 2 emulator.

The driving scenes now run at 30 frames a second, up from about 8. On foot, he walks 1.5 times as fast and the hit detection is fairer. There are also fewer roadblocks and collisions do less damage, which takes a little of the edge off. For the most part the game is as it always was, just with much smoother driving and faster walking.

WHY THE DRIVING IS ABOUT 9% SLOWER ON AVERAGE

In the original, the game moves forward one step each time it finishes drawing a frame, so its speed depends on its frame rate. A frame took anywhere from 6 to 11 sixtieths of a second to draw, which is between 10 and about 5.5 frames a second. Quiet stretches of road ran faster and busy ones slowed down.

With the SA-1 drawing the frames, that link had to be cut, or the game would have run about four times too fast. The patch shows 30 frames a second and moves the game forward once every four of them, which is 7.5 steps a second, all the time.

The first drive averaged about 8.2 steps a second on the original, so it's about 9% slower overall. Its quietest moments are up to 25% slower, and the busiest moments in the game are faster than they were. The attract demo, which is full of enemies and explosions, is only about 2% slower.

WHY ONE STEP EVERY 8

At 30 frames a second, a new frame comes every 2 sixtieths of a second, so a step has to last 6, 8 or 10 of them to be a whole number of frames. 6 would make the first drive about 22% faster and busy scenes about 33% faster, which would make the game harder. 10 would make it drag, at about 27% slower on the first drive. 8 is the closest to how the original plays.

7 is only possible by alternating steps of 3 and 4 frames. That would be closer on the first drive, but the car's speed on screen would keep shifting, and busy scenes would run about 14% faster than they used to.

TESTING

This has only been tested in emulators: Mesen 2, bsnes-mercury and Snes9x. It hasn't been tried on a real SNES or a flash cart.
